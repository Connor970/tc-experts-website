Transaction Coordinator for Wholesalers: What They Handle and Why It Pays

November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Every wholesaler knows the feeling. You finally lock up a deal, find a buyer, and sign the assignment. Then the real work starts: chasing the title company, tracking the option period, answering the seller's calls, and hoping the earnest money got receipted. None of it makes you money directly, but any piece of it going wrong can kill your assignment fee.

That is exactly the work a transaction coordinator for wholesalers takes off your plate. Here is what a TC actually does on a wholesale deal, when it makes sense to bring one on, and what to look for before you hand over your files.

What is a transaction coordinator in wholesaling?

A transaction coordinator (TC) manages a deal from signed contract to closing. On a traditional retail sale, that usually means working with agents and lenders. Wholesale and investor deals are different. They move faster, often involve cash or hard-money buyers, and come with paperwork most retail TCs rarely see, like assignment agreements and double closings.

A TC who specializes in investor deals understands that your contract is only as good as the timeline behind it. Their job is to make sure every party, document and deadline lines up so the deal actually reaches the closing table.

What a TC handles on a wholesale deal

Opening title and sending the contract

As soon as you are under contract, your TC opens title, sends over the purchase agreement and any assignment, and confirms the title company has what it needs. This sounds simple, but a missing page or unsigned addendum is one of the most common reasons files sit untouched for days.

Earnest money tracking

Your TC confirms when earnest money is due, follows up with the buyer, and makes sure title receipts it. Late EMD can give a seller an easy way out of the contract, so this one matters.

Deadline and calendar management

Option or inspection periods, financing deadlines, title objection dates and the closing date all get tracked in one place. A good TC sends reminders before deadlines hit, not after.

Title, lien and HOA research

Wholesale properties often come with baggage: unpaid taxes, code liens, judgments, mortgages in default or HOA balances. Your TC flags these early so you can renegotiate, bring in a payoff or plan around them, instead of finding out the week of closing.

Communication with every party

Sellers, end buyers, title officers, lenders and sometimes attorneys all need updates. A TC becomes the single point of contact, so you are not fielding status calls all day, and you get one clear update on where every file stands.

Closing prep

Your TC reviews the settlement statement for your assignment fee, confirms signing appointments, and makes sure funds and documents are in place before closing day.

Assignments vs. double closes: where a TC earns their keep

Most wholesale deals close one of two ways, and each one has its own coordination headaches.

Assignment of contractDouble close
How it worksYou assign your purchase contract to the end buyer for a fee.You buy the property (A to B), then resell it (B to C), often the same day.
PaperworkPurchase agreement plus assignment agreement.Two purchase contracts, two settlement statements and often transactional funding.
What a TC watchesAssignment fee on the settlement statement, buyer EMD, title's assignment requirements.Timing of both closings, funding for the A-to-B leg, title ready for back-to-back closings.
Common snagTitle company that will not close assignments, or a fee the seller sees and objects to.Funding or title delays on the first leg that push back the second.

A TC who has run both types knows which title companies are investor-friendly, what each one needs, and how to keep both sides of a double close on the same clock.

Signs it is time to outsource transaction coordination

  • You have three or more deals under contract at once and are losing track of dates.
  • You spend more time on follow-up calls than on acquisitions or dispositions.
  • A deal has already fallen through over a missed deadline, late EMD or surprise lien.
  • You want to scale but cannot add more deals without adding more hours.
  • You are thinking about hiring and want to avoid a full-time salary for part-time work.

If any of those sound familiar, the math usually favors outsourcing. Your time is worth far more on the phone with motivated sellers than it is on hold with a title company.

Outsourced TC vs. doing it yourself vs. hiring in-house

Doing it yourself costs nothing upfront, but it costs you deal flow. Every hour on paperwork is an hour not spent marketing, negotiating or selling.

Hiring in-house gives you control, but you take on a salary, training and management, even in slow months.

An outsourced transaction coordinator sits in the middle. You get an experienced TC who already knows investor deals, and you typically pay per file. When deal volume goes up or down, your cost follows it.

The best part for wholesalers: on most wholesale deals, the TC fee is paid by the end buyer at closing. It shows up as a line item on the buyer's side of the settlement statement, which means transaction coordination is typically free to you as the wholesaler.

Quick tip: Before hiring any TC, ask how many assignments and double closes they have coordinated, which title companies they work with, and how they will update you. Specific answers are a good sign.

What to look for in a TC for wholesale and investor deals

  1. Investor-deal experience. Retail-only TCs may not be familiar with assignments, double closes or creative structures like subject-to.
  2. Title research skills. They should know how to dig into liens, payoffs and HOA balances, not just forward emails.
  3. Clear communication. Look for a set update rhythm and one point of contact.
  4. Simple pricing. Flat, per-file pricing paid at closing, ideally structured so the end buyer covers it on wholesale deals.
  5. Speed. Wholesale deals move fast. Your TC should open title and send documents the same day you hand off a file.

Frequently asked questions

Do wholesalers need a transaction coordinator?

Not on day one. But once you have more than a couple of deals under contract at the same time, a TC keeps title, deadlines and paperwork moving so you can stay focused on finding and selling deals.

Can a transaction coordinator handle double closings?

Yes. An experienced TC can coordinate both legs of a double close with the title company, including the A-to-B and B-to-C contracts, funding and closing timelines.

Who pays for a transaction coordinator on a wholesale deal?

Most charge a flat fee per file that is paid at closing. On wholesale deals, that fee is typically paid by the end buyer, so the wholesaler usually pays nothing out of pocket for transaction coordination.

The bottom line

Wholesaling is a numbers game, and the back end of every deal eats into the time you need for the front end. Handing your files to a transaction coordinator who understands wholesale and investor deals lets you protect each assignment fee and spend your hours where they pay the most: finding the next deal. And since the end buyer typically covers the TC fee, you get that help without cutting into your spread.
